# Internal enterprise software

Marketing, SEO and public legal pages usually do not apply. Access control, integration with company systems, adoption and handover carry the weight. Employee data is still personal data, so category A applies to it.

- [ ] **INT1 [CRITICAL] Use the company's single sign-on.** Authenticate through the identity provider, with no separate local passwords except a documented emergency account. Why: central login means central control and removal. Verify: sign in through the identity provider, and confirm a disabled directory account can no longer sign in.
- [ ] **INT2 [CRITICAL] Automate joiners, movers and leavers.** Grant and remove access from directory groups, and remove access when someone leaves or changes role. Why: access that outlives the role is the main internal risk. Verify: remove a test user from the group and confirm access ends.
- [ ] **INT3 [CRITICAL] Define roles and approve them with the business owner.** Apply least privilege and separation of duties for sensitive actions such as approving payments. Why: internal tools often hold the most sensitive data with the weakest controls. Verify: a role matrix is signed off and tested.
- [ ] **INT4 [HIGH] Keep the tool off the public internet unless required.** Place it behind the corporate network, a zero-trust proxy or a VPN. Why: internal tools are built with internal assumptions. Verify: the address is unreachable from an outside network.
- [ ] **INT5 [HIGH] Pass the company's security and architecture review.** Why: most organisations require it before production use, and it has lead time. Verify: the approval is recorded.
- [ ] **INT6 [HIGH] Log access and changes for audit.** Keep the logs for the period policy requires and send them to the central log system. Why: auditors and investigations need the record. Verify: an action by a test user appears in the central logs.
- [ ] **INT7 [HIGH] Test integrations with the systems of record.** Cover HR, finance, CRM and directory integrations, including failure and reconciliation. Why: internal tools mostly move data between other systems. Verify: a change in the source system appears correctly, and a failed sync is reported.
- [ ] **INT8 [HIGH] Migrate data from the old tool or spreadsheet and plan the cutover.** Decide the freeze time, run the migration, verify it, and keep the old system readable for a period. Why: cutover is where data is lost and users lose confidence. Verify: counts and samples match, and the fallback has been rehearsed.
- [ ] **INT9 [HIGH] Run user acceptance testing with real users.** Why: the people doing the job find the gaps. Verify: named users sign off the main workflows.
- [ ] **INT10 [HIGH] Train users and communicate the change.** Provide a guide, a short session or video, and an announcement with the date and what changes for them. Why: an internal tool nobody adopts has failed. Verify: the announcement is sent and the guide is linked in the tool.
- [ ] **INT11 [HIGH] Agree who owns and supports the tool.** Name the business owner, the technical owner, the support route and the hours. Why: internal tools without owners decay. Verify: the owners are recorded in the service catalogue or equivalent.
- [ ] **INT12 [HIGH] Meet employee data and works council requirements.** If: the tool processes employee data or monitors staff. Why: employee monitoring and data are regulated in many jurisdictions. Verify: HR and legal have reviewed it. Needs: A2.
- [ ] **INT13 [MEDIUM] Check compatibility with managed devices and browsers.** Why: corporate machines have locked-down settings. Verify: test on a standard company device.
- [ ] **INT14 [MEDIUM] Follow company policies for data classification, retention and backup.** Why: internal policy applies even where no law does. Verify: the data classification is recorded and the backup follows the standard.
- [ ] **INT15 [MEDIUM] Plan the retirement of what this replaces.** Why: running two systems doubles the work and splits the data. Verify: a date is set to switch off the old tool.
